# Accepting e-waste & electronics donations — two paths

_Research date: 2026-09-05. Nevada County, California. Not legal advice; the
regulatory citations below were confirmed against the primary text where a
source is linked, and marked **(verify)** where only a secondary summary was
reachable._

TechEMPOWER wants to take electronics of any age and condition — including
every kind of lithium battery, e-mobility (e-bikes, scooters, hoverboards) and
EVs — reuse what works and recycle the rest. Two ways to run that, in order of
ambition. Path 1 is the way to start this month; Path 2 is the way to become the
county's contractor.

---

## The one distinction everything hinges on

**Reuse is not waste. Recycling is.**

- A device you accept **to refurbish and give away** is a _donated product_,
  not hazardous waste. No DTSC paperwork attaches to it. Wipe it, fix it, give
  it away — that's Goodwill's model.
- A device you accept **knowing it's going to a recycler** is _universal waste_
  under California's Universal Waste Rule (22 CCR Div. 4.5, Ch. 23). The moment
  you take it from someone else's premises for that purpose you are a
  **universal waste handler accepting from offsite**, and the notification and
  handling rules below apply.

"Any condition" means we will be doing both. The trick is to structure the
recycling half so someone else is the handler of record (Path 1) or to become a
proper handler ourselves (Path 2).

---

## Path 1 — small and fun

**Model:** TechEMPOWER is a _refurbisher and a fundraiser host_, never the
handler of record for the waste stream.

### How it works

1. **Reuse stream (ours):** accept working / repairable computers, phones,
   tablets, e-bikes, etc. Wipe, refurbish, give to families. Keep a simple
   intake log (donor, date, items, serials wiped). Give the donor a written
   acknowledgment (see _Receipts_ below).
2. **Recycle stream (theirs):** don't accumulate dead gear. Instead:
   - **Host e-waste fundraiser days** with a CalRecycle-approved
     collector/recycler. They bring the truck, bins and staff, file the DTSC
     Notice of Intent for the event, weigh the load and pay the nonprofit a
     per-pound rebate on covered devices. We advertise and show up. This is
     what schools and churches do; e.g. [Electronic Recycling Association's
     fundraiser program](https://electronicreusingassociation.us/electronic-waste-fundraiser/),
     [E-Waste Recovery Systems (West Sac/Rancho Cordova, runs NorCal events)](https://www.ewaste411.com/how-does-ers-handle-e-waste/),
     [Tri Valley Recycling (free pickup w/ 3+ items, Sacramento metro)](https://www.trivalleyrecycling.com/).
     Ask each: do _they_ file the NOI and hold the DTSC handler ID for the
     event; do they take battery-embedded products and loose lithium; what is
     the per-pound rate on CEW (screens) vs. everything else.
   - Between events, point dead-battery and broken-screen donors straight to
     the county: **McCourtney Road Transfer Station, 14741 Wolf Mountain Rd,
     Grass Valley.** E-waste & universal waste Wed–Sun 8:00–3:30; household
     hazardous waste (incl. damaged batteries) Fri–Sun 8:00–3:30, households
     only. WM (530) 274-3090 · County Solid Waste (530) 265-7111.
     ([County transfer stations page](https://www.nevadacountyca.gov/3412/Transfer-Stations),
     [WM HHW hours](https://localsites.wm.com/a4480000006oNvgAAE/Nevada+City+McCourtney+Road+Household+Hazardous+Waste+Facility),
     [County battery-recycling notice](https://www.nevadacountyca.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/8516))

### What still applies even on Path 1

- **Don't advertise a standing public drop-off address.** Advertising a
  drop-off location/date is exactly what DTSC defines as a _drop-off event_,
  which requires an NOI filed **≥30 days before** the event by the collector or
  handler (CalRecycle LCC guide, p. "Drop-off Events"; 22 CCR §66273.32(c)(1)).
  The site copy therefore says "email us and we'll arrange it," not "bring it
  to the barn."
- **Lithium batteries in the trash are illegal in CA** (SB 244, PRC §42990 —
  [bill text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202120220SB244)).
  The safety callout on the site tells donors to tape terminals / bag cells and
  to take swollen or damaged packs to HHW, not to us.
- **Lithium at the barn is the real hazard, not the regulator.** Unincorporated
  Nevada County is high fire severity. Even a small reuse stock of e-bike and
  laptop packs should be: on concrete, in a metal cabinet or ammo-can style
  bins, away from the structure's exit and from anything flammable, no
  charging unattended, a Class D / lithium-rated extinguisher or a bucket of
  sand nearby. Tell the landlord (your sister) and check the renter's/property
  insurance — most homeowner policies exclude "business use" storage and
  hazardous materials. This is the item most likely to bite.
- **Data.** Wipe every drive before reuse (NIST 800-88 "purge": `nvme
sanitize` / `hdparm --security-erase` / `blkdiscard`, or shred the platters).
  Keep a wipe log with serials. Offer a data-destruction confirmation on
  request — the site promises this. Locked iPhones/Macs (Activation Lock)
  cannot be reused; the site tells donors to sign out first.

### Copper wire, electrical hardware, bulbs

- **Scrap metal (copper wire, conduit, enclosures, panels, motors, breakers)
  is not universal waste** — it's an exempt recyclable scrap metal stream
  (22 CCR §66261.6(a)(3)(B) — **verify**). No DTSC paperwork; strip and sell
  to a scrap yard (bare bright copper is the highest-value thing anyone will
  ever hand us). Keep a receipt log for the books.
- **Smart bulbs, LED fixtures, dimmers, smart switches** contain circuit
  boards → treat as electronic devices (same rules as everything else above).
- **Fluorescent tubes and CFLs are universal-waste _lamps_ (mercury).** Don't
  accept them; the site tells donors to take them to McCourtney Road HHW.
  Broken tubes are a cleanup problem we don't want in a barn.
- **We don't accept alkaline, NiCad, NiMH or lead-acid batteries** (JP's
  call, 2026-09-05) — no resale value, they're the bulk of the weight, and
  every auto-parts store and the transfer station takes them free. The site
  says so; point donors to McCourtney Road.

### Receipts (both paths)

- Nonprofit gives a **written acknowledgment** describing the items (no value
  — the donor sets fair market value). For any single gift ≥$250 the IRS
  requires a contemporaneous written acknowledgment for the donor to deduct.
- Donor files **Form 8283** if total noncash gifts for the year exceed $500;
  over **$5,000** for an item/group the donor needs a qualified appraisal and
  the nonprofit signs Section B.
  ([IRS: About Form 8283](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8283))
- **Vehicles** (anything titled: cars, golf carts on-road, motorcycles):
  nonprofit must furnish **Form 1098-C** within **30 days** of the
  contribution (if we keep/use it) or of the sale (if we sell it), and file
  Copy A with the IRS; without it the donor is capped at a $500 deduction.
  ([IRS vehicle-donation guidance](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/irs-guidance-explains-rules-for-vehicle-donations),
  [Instructions for 1098-C](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1098c)).
  California side: donor files the DMV **Notice of Transfer and Release of
  Liability (REG 138) within 5 days**; we do the title transfer. If we ever
  _sell_ donated vehicles as a program, California's AG treats third-party
  vehicle-donation operations as commercial fundraisers — do it ourselves,
  don't outsource to a "cars for charity" middleman without checking the
  Registry of Charitable Trusts rules.
- E-bikes, scooters, hoverboards, mobility scooters are **not** titled
  vehicles → ordinary noncash gifts (8283 rules), not 1098-C.

### Thrift stores as a supply line (Path 1 today, revenue in Path 2)

Every thrift store in western Nevada County (Hospice of the Foothills Gift &
Thrift, Salvation Army, Animal Save, Habitat ReStore, the church shops) gets
electronics they can't test, can't wipe, can't sell, and — with SB 244 — can't
legally throw away if there's a battery inside. Most either pay a hauler or
quietly dumpster it. Offer them:

- **A scheduled free pickup** of everything electronic they don't want. Their
  disposal cost goes to zero; our intake goes up without a single public
  drop-off event (no NOI needed — the store is the donor, we're arranging a
  pickup, same as any household).
- **Test-and-wipe for the sellable stuff**, returned with a "wiped & working"
  sticker so they can price it with confidence. Later: a revenue share or a
  flat per-unit fee — this is a real service line once we're certified.
- **Lithium triage.** We take the e-bikes, hoverboards, power tools and
  laptop packs that are their biggest fire and liability worry.

Their donors also get our receipts indirectly: a thrift store that can say
"your old laptop went to a family through TechEMPOWER" is a better pitch than
"we threw it away."

### Path 1 checklist

- [ ] Intake log + acknowledgment template (Google Sheet is fine)
- [ ] Wipe procedure written down; wipe log with serials
- [ ] Battery storage: concrete floor, metal bin, extinguisher/sand, no
      unattended charging; landlord + insurance conversation
- [ ] Line up one recycler partner; book the first fundraiser day (they file
      the NOI; confirm in writing)
- [ ] 1098-C workflow before accepting the first titled vehicle (we can e-file
      through any 1099 service)

## Path 2 — the certified thing (become the county's processor)

**Model:** TechEMPOWER is a DTSC-notified universal waste handler and a
CalRecycle **Approved Collector**, paid per pound for covered devices, able to
bid the county's e-waste hauling/processing contract.

### Ladder, in order

1. **DTSC Notice of Intent (NOI)** — file electronically at
   [hwts.dtsc.ca.gov/uwed](https://hwts.dtsc.ca.gov/uwed/) **≥30 calendar days
   before** first accepting any electronic device / CRT from offsite
   (22 CCR §66273.32(c)(1), [text](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/22-CCR-66273.32)).
   The form asks the types of devices, the sources, and whether you might
   accumulate ≥5,000 kg at once (§66273.32(c)(2)(H)–(J)). Once you accept
   > 100 kg/yr from offsite you owe DTSC an **annual report by Feb 1**
   > (**verify** the current threshold on DTSC's
   > [e-waste annual report FAQ](https://dtsc.ca.gov/e-waste-annual-report-frequently-asked-questions-faqs/)).
2. **Handler housekeeping** (22 CCR Ch. 23, Art. 3 — **verify** section
   numbers against current text):
   - Label containers "Universal Waste — Electronic Devices" / "— Batteries"
     with the accumulation start date; **1-year accumulation limit** from
     receipt.
   - Batteries: terminals taped or each cell individually bagged; store in
     closed, structurally sound containers; damaged/defective lithium
     segregated in non-combustible containment.
   - **Annual universal-waste training** for everyone who touches the stream,
     70%+ on DTSC's two UW tests, records kept **3 years on site**
     (CalRecycle LCC guide + Human-I-T summary — **verify** the test/score
     detail on DTSC's training page).
   - Ship only to another handler or a destination facility; keep shipment
     records (the CalRecycle **198 log** does double duty) 3 years.
3. **CalRecycle Approved Collector** — apply through CEWIS
   ([forms](https://calrecycle.ca.gov/electronics/formsandinfo/)). Gets us paid
   per pound for **Covered Electronic Waste** (screens: TVs, monitors, laptops,
   tablets — and, since SB 1215 took effect 1 Jan 2026, **battery-embedded
   products** are being phased into the CEW program, with a 1.5%/≤$15 consumer
   fee funding it —
   [US News](https://www.usnews.com/news/best-states/california/articles/2026-01-02/new-california-fee-seeks-to-increase-collection-recycling-of-lithium-batteries),
   [TechWaste summary of SB 1215 timeline](https://www.techwasterecycling.com/california-sb-1215-battery-embedded-products/)).
   Obligations: 198 logs on every transfer to an Approved Recycler; update
   CEWIS within 10 days of contact changes / 30 days of address change; ship at
   least one load per year or the ID goes inactive (then a 90-day blackout).
   **Designated Approved Collector** status is how a local government
   authorizes a collector to work its territory — that's the doorway to the
   county contract.
4. **CUPA & local** — Nevada County Environmental Health is the Certified
   Unified Program Agency that actually inspects universal-waste handlers.
   Call them before filing the NOI; they'll tell you what they inspect.
   For the barn: the county's **LUDC zoning** almost certainly requires a use
   permit for a recycling collection facility on an AG/RA parcel, and the
   landlord must sign. A rented residential barn is a fine _reuse workshop_;
   as a permanent _waste consolidation site_ it's a permit and an insurance
   problem. Plan Path 2's consolidation point in an industrial/commercial
   zone (Whispering Pines, Loma Rica Dr., Penn Valley industrial) or on
   county property as part of the contract.
5. **Fire code** — California Fire Code Ch. 12 (§1207) sets thresholds for
   stored lithium-ion; below the thresholds it's still "hazardous materials
   storage" for the Fire Marshal. Get the Grass Valley/Nevada County Fire
   Marshal to walk the site once.
6. **Certification** (the thing procurement actually asks for) — **R2v3** or
   **e-Stewards**. Both require: documented downstream chain of custody to
   final disposition, data-sanitization procedures (NIST 800-88), EHS
   management system, insurance, closure plan. Realistic for a small
   nonprofit only as a _collector_ under a certified recycler's downstream
   (an "R2 Ready" tier), not as a processor, in year one. Many counties
   accept "collector transferring exclusively to R2/e-Stewards-certified
   recyclers" in an RFP.
7. **Data destruction as a paid service** — the county also pays an outside
   vendor to destroy its HDDs/SSDs. What procurement asks for here is
   **NAID AAA certification** (i-SIGMA) — the recognized standard for secure
   destruction vendors — plus: NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 1 methods (purge/destroy:
   ATA Secure Erase, NVMe sanitize, crypto-erase, degauss for magnetic, shred
   for SSDs/flash since degaussing does nothing to them), per-serial
   **certificate of destruction**, chain-of-custody log from pickup to
   destruction, locked transport bins, background-checked staff, and
   liability insurance (typically $1–2M, with a cyber/privacy rider).
   Government data may also be subject to CJIS (sheriff/courts) and HIPAA
   (health & human services) — those departments will want witnessed
   destruction on site, which is where a **mobile shredder** (or a
   crusher/puncher for SSDs) pays for itself. Start cheap: a documented
   software-wipe + drill/press-destroy procedure and a serial-logged
   certificate template is enough for small businesses and nonprofits now;
   NAID AAA is the year-two goal that unlocks the county bid.
8. **Cal/OSHA** — once there are employees or regular volunteers handling
   CRTs/batteries: IIPP, hazard communication, PPE, lifting.
9. **The county deal** — Nevada County pays a SoCal firm to truck e-waste out.
   With the NOI filed, Approved Collector status, a documented certified
   downstream and a local consolidation site, TechEMPOWER can propose to be
   the county's **Designated Approved Collector**: local pickup and
   consolidation, CEW payments partly offsetting the county's cost, reuse
   stream feeding low-income families (a story the Board of Supervisors will
   like). Ask purchasing for the current contract's scope, term and renewal
   date first — you're bidding against an incumbent's renewal, so time the
   proposal to it.

### Path 2 checklist

- [ ] Call Nevada County Environmental Health (CUPA) — what they inspect
- [ ] File DTSC NOI (30-day clock)
- [ ] Written UW procedures: labeling, 1-yr clock, battery containment,
      training + test records
- [ ] CalRecycle Approved Collector application; CEWIS login; 198 log routine
- [ ] Choose an Approved Recycler that is R2v3/e-Stewards; get downstream
      documentation in writing
- [ ] Site: industrial/commercial zone or county property; Fire Marshal walk
- [ ] Insurance: general liability + pollution rider
- [ ] Pull the county's current e-waste AND data-destruction contracts (scope/term) from purchasing
- [ ] Data destruction: written NIST 800-88 procedure, serial-logged certificate template, chain-of-custody form; NAID AAA application as a year-two goal
